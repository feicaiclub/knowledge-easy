# Living brush / 生长笔触

Use shaped strokes, pigment density and visible bristles to make structure emerge. Stroke direction follows the object: a leaf tapers, a trunk thickens, water branches, cloth folds.

Build stable geometry with seeded variation. Animate the growth or transformation of that geometry; do not regenerate a different painting every frame. Let layered translucency provide depth without turning the whole picture muddy. The palette can be botanical, mineral, monochrome or another coherent choice suited to the film.

A brush can reveal a process as well as a landscape. Follow a branching system, move from a surface to its hidden structure, or let one stroke become a new object. Camera and pacing come from the subject. Not every film needs a plant, mountain or slow meditative rhythm.

For detailed forms, compose families of strokes with different widths, opacity and direction. Keep a clear large structure before adding small marks. Tiny isolated leaves on a mostly empty frame are not a substitute for an intentional composition.

Sound may emphasize breath, water, surface contact or a sparse acoustic texture, but it should follow the world and the change being shown. Reading time and material movement matter more than a fixed tempo.

Variation space: a topographic system, a living cross-section, an object study, an imagined environment, an illustrated memory or a process. Use the same brush tools to create different subjects and spatial arrangements.

Implementation: `artwork` with `style: "wash"`; `art.brush` and `stroke`. Cache time-independent stroke geometry, and derive reveal progress from `t` or the shared beats. Inspect stroke detail, color layers and the completed form, not just the first reveal. [Guide](../illustration.md).
