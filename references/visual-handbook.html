01 / TYPOGRAPHY & COLOR

Give every word
a clear role.

English and Chinese share the same design system, with different fonts and line breaks. Keep the hierarchy clear without forcing one language into the other's measure.

Look closely.
Think again.
EVIDENCE / CONTEXT / JUDGMENT
Display

Source Serif 4 for English. Noto Serif SC for Chinese. Use a few meaningful words as the visual anchor.

Explanation

Inter for English. Noto Sans SC for Chinese. Keep supporting copy next to the image or relationship it explains.

Numbers

Barlow Condensed for dates, counts, and chapter numbers. Make the unit and meaning clear.

Captions

Break English at phrase boundaries. Use one line for Chinese subtitles and at most two for English. Split long speech into separate phrase-timed cues; do not shrink the type.

Ink & gold

Questions, concepts, and judgments. Deep photographic shadows and warm points of emphasis.

Wine & jade

Conflict and comparison. Color separates ideas without automatically declaring a winner.

Paper & terrain

Evidence, history, and maps. Give land, routes, places, and dates distinct visual roles.

02 / BASE DESIGNS

Make the material
part of the explanation.

Start with the missing piece: a subject, a distinction, a relationship, a sequence, or evidence. Call each base design directly from film.json, replace its content, and set the timing for the actual script. Combine instances by region or time through the production entry.

Choose the reasoning

Show a cycle with a feedback loop, compare records with an evidence collage, or widen a detail into its context. Use the complete composition as a baseline. Custom designs must retain comparable subjects, material, hierarchy and motion; inspect their exported clips beside this handbook. There is no variety quota.

Keep the world moving

Explanatory graphics sit over graded, softly blurred playing footage. Preserve enough motion and detail to read as video. Full-frame maps, documents, and interludes need a clear editorial reason.

Give lines their own space

Place images, labels, and connector lanes together. A picture must not hide an edge or arrowhead. Re-route around obstacles instead of fixing a bad path with paint order.

03 / HISTORY & GEOGRAPHY

Every story
happens somewhere.

Use geography to establish place, explain distance, trace a route, or compare an extent. All layers share a coordinate system.

Map focus

Establish where the story takes place.

Move from region to place using one coordinate system for the camera and labels.

Routes and connections

Show direction, stops, and order.

Grow the route in segments and label each arrival. Animation time is not travel time.

Period comparison

Compare spatial change on the same geographic base.

Keep the scale and viewport fixed. Distinguish uncertain boundaries.

Build the setting

Show the land, rivers, and places the explanation needs. Add terrain only when it affects the story.

Name the period

Separate historical and modern names. Distinguish borders, administrative areas, influence, and control.

Keep the scale

Use matching projections and viewports for comparisons. Mark illustrative routes and uncertain boundaries honestly.

05 / LAYOUT, MOTION & REVIEW

The details
carry the quality.

Settle the composition before animating it. Then watch how it changes over time, with the real narration and score.

Layout

Align related objects, balance image and text, and center conclusions on the group they summarize. Keep lines level and route connectors clear of images, labels, and arrowheads. Reserve the lower caption band; information stays inside x=150–1770 and y=80–830 at 1920×1080.

Purpose

A component should add understanding. Choose the form for the reasoning rather than implementation convenience. Omit redundant labels, decorative diagrams, and graphics that reveal the conclusion too early.

Language

Keep words together and break at meaningful phrases. Avoid tiny type, stranded punctuation, and a lone word on the last line.

Footage

Grade shots consistently. Use each original moving shot once in a production film, including blurred backgrounds and insets. Exclude smoking shots.

CheckWhat to look for
EntranceThe subject is clear, the first words are intact, and a reveal does not arrive before its meaning.
HoldCopy is readable. Group centers and line endpoints stay stable. Background motion remains perceptible; no image covers a connector or crowds a label.
TransitionWatch the moving state as well as the start and end. Check cards as they cross the frame, arrowheads, captions, and safe edges. Avoid clipping, flashes, and abrupt audio joins.
ExportWatch and listen to the actual MP4. A screenshot, transcript, or numeric check cannot establish the whole experience.

A consistent narrator, a responsive performance

Use the approved D reference and body-v1 settings. Design emphasis, pace, pauses, and emotion for each story. English and Chinese use the same voice reference, with separate language settings and pronunciation review.
